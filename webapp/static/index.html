CityFragility

Measuring how road closures reshape access to mapped healthcare — central Karachi

– nodes – edges – mapped facilities – closure segments research mode

When a road closes, how far does “the nearest hospital” move?

CityFragility is a local research tool built on real OpenStreetMap data of central Karachi. It answers one narrow question with measured numbers: if a road segment is hypothetically closed, how do shortest drivable distances from sample locations to the nearest mapped healthcare facility change?

Every number on this site was measured once on the real road graph and saved to files. Nothing here is generated by an AI, and nothing is simulated “for effect”. The findings tabs read those saved results directly.

Verified research scale

  • Network: driving graph extracted from OpenStreetMap via Geofabrik pakistan-latest.osm.pbf (snapshot 2026-10-09, ODbL licence).
  • Study area: central Karachi — CORE ~10 × 11 km, BUFFER ~15 × 17 km. This is not whole-city coverage.
  • Experiment: 100 grid-sampled origins × 100 real OSM ways, run once and stored; the explorer reads the stored results.
  • Validation: exact re-run reproducibility, an independent routing-engine cross-check, sampling sensitivity and a facility data-quality audit (see the Findings tab).

What this model CAN say

  • For these 100 sample locations, the measured detour (extra drivable metres) or disconnection caused by closing these 100 ways.
  • Which sampled ways most affect sampled access to mapped facilities — under a transparent composite formula, with separate rankings also available.
  • How sensitive those rankings are to how origins were sampled (they are sensitive — see Findings).
  • Reproducible methodology: every step is scripted, tested and re-runnable.

What this model CANNOT say

  • Not real flood or disaster risk. Closures are hypothetical “what-ifs”, not predictions of any event.
  • Not verified emergency care. “Hospital” here means an OpenStreetMap mapper tag. No health registry confirmed 24/7 emergency or trauma capability.
  • Not population impact. The 100 origins are a geographic grid sample — they are not population-weighted, not patient homes, not representative of people.
  • Not citywide. Conclusions apply only to the bounded central-Karachi study area and this bounded set of tested roads.
  • No ambulance, casualty or traffic claims. The model measures distance on the driving graph only — no times, no congestion, no turn restrictions, no outcomes.

Three ways to explore

  1. Explorer — browse the stored vulnerability experiment: ranked roads, measured effects, filters, and a map of the tested segments.
  2. Simulator — run a live single-closure simulation on the real graph (Phase 1 workflow, including the verified A Street +442.27 m regression).
  3. Findings — read the validated research results in plain language, with limitations stated up front.

How to use — 3 steps

  1. Pick a road from the dropdown (or click a highlighted segment on the map).
  2. Press Simulate Closure — every directed edge of that OSM way is removed, shortest drivable routes are recomputed, then the graph is restored exactly.
  3. Compare routes — blue = original path, dashed pink = alternative after closure; click any origin row to switch the map view.

The first simulation loads the real 45k-node graph into server memory once (~5–6 s); later runs take well under a second.

1 · Select a road segment

Bounded set of real OSM ways (stable osmid identifiers). Options marked tested were already verified in Phase 0. You can also click a highlighted candidate on the map.

2 · Simulate & restore

Simulation removes every directed edge of the selected OSM way (one-way restrictions preserved), recomputes shortest drivable distances from the six sample origins to the nearest mapped facility, then restores the graph exactly. These six origins are illustrative Phase 0 locations — not a population sample.

Provenance & limitations

  • Road network & facilities: OpenStreetMap contributors, via Geofabrik pakistan-latest.osm.pbf (snapshot 2026-10-09). ODbL license. sha256 a125c80a…a627b
  • Study area: central Karachi — CORE ~10×11 km, BUFFER ~15×17 km (W,S,E,N = 66.970, 24.770, 67.120, 24.920).
  • Facilities are unverified. “Hospital” is an OSM mapper tag — it does not confirm 24/7 emergency or trauma capability. No official health registry was checked.
  • Hypothetical closures only. This tool does not predict real floods, road accidents, ambulance arrival times, traffic, or citywide population impacts.
  • Model limits: distance-based routing on the driving graph; one-ways honoured; no turn restrictions, signals, or congestion. Origin results are sample-only.
  • Engine: Phase 0 NetworkX pipeline (45,304 nodes / 122,599 directed edges), 8/8 routing tests passing; graph restored exactly after every simulation.
